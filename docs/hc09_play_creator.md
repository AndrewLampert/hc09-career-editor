# Plays as data: the play creator prototype

What a play is in the game's files, what the editor's **Play creator** shows and builds from it, and what is known (and not known) about writing a custom play outside the game. Companion documents: `hc09_play_data.md` (sets and personnel), `hc09_custom_plays.md` (custom plays and playbooks in a save).

**Status: prototype.** Reading and drawing the game's plays is checked against the game's own diagrams. Making a custom play writes rows that follow what the in-game Create-A-Play wrote, passes the bridge's structure check and reads back correctly, but **it has not been tried in the game yet** (a test plan is at the end).

## A play, table by table

All of this is in the master play database (`qkl_boot.ast` entry 40; `qkl_fe2ig.ast` 1674 to 1679 hold subsets, see `hc09_play_data.md`).

| Table | One row is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `PBPL` | a play: `PLYL` (id), `SETL` (the set it is run from), `PLYT` (play type), `name`, `motn` (1 on 3,075 of the 3,099 plays, so it is not a motion flag; what it is is not known), `PLF_`, `risk`, `vpos`, `SITT` | 3,099 plays: 2,513 offense, 549 defense, 37 special teams |
| `SETL` / `SETP` / `SETG` / `SGFF` | the set (alignment): 11 `SETP` slots with `poso`, `EPos`, `DPos`, `artx` / `arty` (where each lines up in the diagram's units, x about 0 to 180, y about 0 to 104); `SETG` the alignments of pre-snap motion variants (floats, in yards, stored as the raw 32 bits), `SGFF` the alignment groups | 240 sets: 185 offense, 41 defense, 14 special teams |
| `PLYS` | one slot of a play: `PSAL` (what the player does), `ARTL` (the route drawing), `PLRR` (a role code), `poso` | 34,089 rows; 11 per play |
| `PSLO` / `PSLD` | the **step lists**: rows (`PSAL`, `step`, `code`, `val1`, `val2`, `val3`), each list ending with code 255; `PSLO` for offense, `PSLD` for defense | 1,138 offense lists, 403 defense lists |
| `ARTO` / `ARTD` | the **route art**: up to 12 numbered records per `ARTL` (`sp`, `ls`, `at`, `ct`, `lt`, `au`, `av`, `ax`, `ay`) | 941 offense, 487 defense |
| `PLPD` | pass plays: the read order (5 x percent + receiver slot) | |
| `PLRD` | run plays: the hole | |
| `PLCM` | play combinations (option decisions) | |
| `PBAI` | which playbook (`BOKL`) holds which play | |

### Id spaces: `PSLO` and `PSLD` overlap

The two step-list tables number their lists separately but share ids: **138 ids (2182 to 2343, not contiguous) are in both tables with identical lists**, and so overlap `ARTO` / `ARTD` in 117 ids. Anything keyed by `PSAL` or `ARTL` alone silently takes the defense table's rows for an offense play. The editor keys everything by side (`hc09_plays.PlayLibrary.steps[side]`, `art_rows[side]`); special-teams sets use whichever table most of their plays' ids are only in (special-teams plays use both tables, some slots from `PSLO` and others from `PSLD`).

**The 138 shared lists are exactly the ones no stock play uses**, on both sides (offense 138 of 1,138 unused, defense 138 of 403). They are short and generic (`15`, `14`, `28`, `29`, `41(24,104,255) 2`, `8(104,32,254) 2` ...). The profile used for testing (the user's own) has 14 custom plays (154 `PLYS` rows) using **60 different `PSAL` ids: 22 of the shared 138 and 38 ordinary ones** (all blocking and route lists that stock plays also use), none of them with option codes. So the in-game Create-A-Play builds from the shared lists *and* from ordinary ones; which exact set it offers per position is not known. Their `ARTL` is not derivable from the `PSAL`: the profile pairs list 2182 with art 233 and list 2183 with art 225 and so on, one art per route shape.

### Step codes

No documentation of these was found (the web has none), so every reading below comes from the data: which positions use a code, whether the play is a pass or a run (it has a `PLPD` or a `PLRD` row), what the play is called (a code found in every "trap" and "sweep" play and nowhere else is a pull), what comes before and after it, and how its numbers are spread. **Sure** means the pattern is clear; **likely** means it fits everything seen but is a reading; **?** is a guess that fits. `hc09_plays.STEP_TERMS` holds them, and the designer shows them in words with a "(?)" after the guesses. The numbers inside a step (`val1` to `val3`) are not decoded, apart from the few remarks below.

The 138 step lists that are in both tables are identical, so the vocabulary is shared by offense and defense; where a code means something different for the quarterback it says so.

| Code | Words | How sure, and why |

Coverage: **99.5% of the offensive and 98.8% of the defensive steps** of the game's step lists get a word, and 1,119 of 1,138 offensive and 390 of 403 defensive lists are read in full; many of the readings are guesses and show a "(?)". The solid part of the table (routes, blocks, zones, rush, man coverage, options, motion) is solid; the quarterback and run-play back sequences are consistent but not proven in the game.

261 offensive plays contain these codes (46 distinct names: Triple Option, Triple Option Rev / Cntr / Pass, Tri(-) Option Pass, Option Pass, Speed Option, Jet Option, QB Slot Option, Power Option, Shovel Option, Quick Option, TE / HB / FB / WR options ...), in 61 step lists. **None of them is among the 138 shared lists and none is in the profile's custom plays**: the in-game editor is reported not to offer option plays, and the game's own plays prove the engine supports them, so they can be assigned in a custom play from the designer.

### The art

Art columns are signed integers of different widths (`ART_BITS` in `hc09_plays.py`, from the table definition); the bridge exports them as raw unsigned numbers, so they are sign-extended when read (an `ay` of 251 in 8 bits is -5). `(ax, ay)` are absolute offsets from the player's art position (`artx`, `arty`); the route is the position followed by each used record (`sp` not 0). `at` 1 is an arrowhead. On defense a record with `ct` 3 is a **zone** (an oval with a line to it): code 23 deep (blue), 22 curl and 20 flat (pink), 21 hook (yellow). Checked against the in-game diagrams: *Pass Deep* gives four deep zones at the top, two curl / flat and a hook; *Slants* WR1 goes up the field and then in (offsets `(0,-24) (0,-26) (-18,-40)`).

## The kinds of offensive play, and what each needs

The quarterback's steps say what kind of play it is, and the linemen, backs and receivers go with it. 2,508 of the 2,513 offensive plays fall into nine kinds this way (the other five are spikes, kneels and a fake field goal). `hc09_playtypes.py` reads them; the numbers below are from the game's 2,508 plays.

| Kind | Stock plays | `PLYT` values (plays) | Quarterback | Linemen | First back | A receiver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dropback pass | 967 | 102 (414), 103 (304), 3 (115), 101 (72) ... | drop back > read and throw (99%) | pass block (98%) | release path > pass block, or a route (20% each) | route: 2 legs, 1 cut, then look for the ball (36%) |
| Play-action pass | 191 | 4 (190) | take the snap and turn > play-action fake > read and throw (92%) | run block > pass block (87%) | go to the mesh point > run through the hole > pass block (27%) | route: 2 legs, 1 cut, then look for the ball (35%) |
| Option pass | 14 | 2 (14) | option start > option read > read and throw (57%) | run block > pass block (71%) | mesh point > option pitch route > lead path (43%) | route: 3 legs, 2 cuts, then look for the ball (61%) |
| Handoff run | 1,003 | 151 (247), 13 (221), 14 (164), 12 (155) ... | take the snap and turn > hand off > carry out the fake (70%) | run block (80%) | mesh point > run through the hole > carry the ball (64%) | lead path > run block (40%) |
| Toss / pitch run | 190 | 15 (185) | pitch > carry out the fake (84%) | run block (67%), lead paths (25%) | mesh point > take the pitch > carry the ball (95%) | route: 1 leg > run block (59%) |
| Speed (read) option | 54 | 17 (53) | go to the mesh point > option read > carry the ball (78%) | run block (100%) | mesh point > option pitch route > pass block (53%) | route: 1 leg > run block (98%) |
| Triple option | 40 | 17 (40) | option start > option read > carry the ball (100%) | run block (100%) | wait > run through the hole x2 > carry the ball (the dive back, 32%) | route: 1 leg > run block (68%) |
| QB run (keeper, sneak, choice) | 42 | 17 (13), 14 (12), 11 (12) ... | option start > drop back > carry the ball (55%) | run block (68%) | wait > run through the hole x2 > carry the ball (55%) | route: 1 leg > run block (47%) |
| Fake run (fake handoff) | 7 | 18 (5), 11 (2) | take the snap and turn > play-action fake > carry out the fake (43%) | run block (89%) | mesh point > take the pitch > carry the ball (57%) | run block (45%) |

Which table holds a read or a hole follows the kind: every pass kind has a `PLPD` row (the read order) and no `PLRD`; the run kinds have a `PLRD` row (the hole) in 98 to 100% of their plays and almost never a `PLPD`. `PLCM` (a play combination) is optional: 29% of dropbacks, 15% of play actions, 14% of handoffs, 30% of triple options.

**Play action against a normal pass** (both are passes with a read order): the quarterback's `3 7` becomes `38 40 7` (turn to the back and fake the handoff, then read and throw); the linemen's pass block `14` becomes run block then pass block `15 14` (87%); the first back's release or route becomes the fake, mesh point and run through the hole, then pass block (or a route); the receivers' routes are the same (97% routes in a dropback, 99% in play action); the `PLYT` is 4 in 190 of 191 play actions against 3, 5 and 101 to 104 in a dropback.

**Triple option against the speed option** (both are runs with a hole and `PLYT` 17): the quarterback's first step is `26` (go to the mesh point) in the speed option and `12` (option start, the ride with the dive back) in the triple option, both then read (`42`) and keep (`1`); in the triple option a back is the dive runner (`25 13 13 1`, wait then run through the hole twice, 22 to 26% of the backs) and another takes the pitch (`26 43 15`) while the speed option has only the pitch man (`26 43 14`, 45%) with the second back blocking; the speed option's receivers block downfield (98%) while 20% of the triple option's receivers run a route; the linemen all run block in both; the triple option has a combination (`PLCM`) twice as often (30% against 15%).

**Wildcat: the game has no such kind.** The only set named `Wildcat` (set 497, a shotgun set with a quarterback, a fullback, a halfback, two receivers and a tight end) has exactly one play, "PA Jet Sweep" (quarterback `12 3 7`, the halfback in motion), which is a play action with motion. The only snap to a back is "RB Direct Snap" (`PLYT` 155, set 71 "FakePuntDirectSnap", a fake punt with no quarterback slot where the fullback runs `3 1`: a path, then carry the ball). One play cannot define "required steps", and who takes the snap is probably decided by the set (the position of the slot where the quarterback stands), not by the steps. The game test that would show it: in a copy of the game files, change slot 0 of a shotgun set from quarterback to halfback with the personnel tool, give that slot the direct-snap steps (`3 1`) and the old halfback's slot the quarterback's, and see who takes the snap.

### What "required" means here

- **The quarterback's steps** must read as the kind (that is how the kind is defined): this is always required.
- **Everyone else** must do something the game ships in that kind for that role (a lineman never runs a route in any stock play, so that is refused). That is the check that says whether the play works: **all 2,508 stock plays pass it in their own kind** by construction, and the number of plays the check refuses in the other kinds is what makes it useful (a pass-blocking lineman is shipped 22 times among 5,015 handoff linemen, a route by a lineman never).
- **Typical for the kind** is stricter and is what a conversion aims for: a behaviour at least 10% of that role's stock slots have (and at least 3 slots); for the linemen the whole step list at least 12% of the kind's linemen have (so a play action's linemen must run block then pass block, not just pass block); for the backs, a first real step (not a wait or a motion step) at least 8% of that role's slots start with (the mesh point, the handoff, the pitch). Receivers' routes count as typical wherever the kind has routes.
- **A conversion** (`PlayTypes.convert`) changes only what does not fit the target kind. It takes the changed players' assignments from the closest stock play of the target kind **in the same set** (the most players already the same, then the same `PLYT`), so the art and the alignment match and the combination is one the game ships; when no stock play of that kind uses the set it uses the kind's most common assignment for each position. It keeps slots you chose by hand unless they could not work, and keeps receivers' routes in the pass kinds. `PLYT` is the base play's if the kind has that value, else the sibling's or the kind's most common; the pass reads (`PLPD`) or the run hole (`PLRD`) come from the sibling or are made (read order from the routes in the order wide receiver, tight end, back, with the kind's median percentages; the hole the base play had).
- **Acceptance** (`playtypes_test.py`, on copies of the game's tables): all 20,064 conversions of a stock play to each of the other eight kinds give a play that the check passes and that reads as the target kind; converting a stock play to its own kind changes nothing (2,508 of 2,508); 7,543 of the conversions copy a stock play of the target kind in the same set and the rest use the kind's usual assignments; a second conversion of a converted play changes nothing.


## Where the players line up (Move players)

The yard values of an alignment are the `SETG` floats of the set's normal group (`x___` across the field, plus to the right; `y___` from the line of scrimmage, minus behind it; the game's offensive sets use x from -17.5 to 17.5 and y from -10.6 to -0.6; set 333's quarterback is at (0.0, -1.4), the halfback at (0.0, -7.0), a wide receiver at (17.5, -2.2)). A custom play has its **own** set in the profile (its own `SETP` and `SETG` rows, copied from the stock set), so **the editor can put a player somewhere else in the new play's rows without touching a game file**: the **Move players...** window of the Play creator lists the eleven players with their yards, lets you drag a player on the field picture or step him by half a yard or type his yards (held to 22 yards either side of the middle and 0.4 to 12 yards behind the line), and **Make custom play** writes the new places.

What is written: the normal group's `x___` / `y___` of the moved player in the new set's `SETG` rows; the flipped place `fx__` / `fy__` of the slot that takes his mirrored place when the play is flipped (2,007 of the 2,035 slots of the game's offensive sets have exactly one such partner: `fx` is minus the partner's `x`, `fy` the partner's `y`; in a symmetric formation a flip changes nothing); and, in the diagram's units, the player's `SETP` `artx` / `arty` / `fmtx` / `fmty` (the picture, which your test showed moves only the thumbnail). **Not changed:** the pre-snap motion groups (`M1ri` ... `M5le`). 72% of their slots stand somewhere else than in the normal group, so one move cannot be copied into them safely, and a play with pre-snap motion (step 45) keeps the motion starting places of the stock set.

The picture squeezes the field (a wide receiver 17.5 yards out is drawn 75 units from the middle, a tackle 3.3 yards out 24), so `YardMap` fits a curve between yards and the picture's units from where the game's own sets stand in both; it puts the game's own players within 2 units of their pictures for half of them and within 6 for nine in ten. The yards are exact; only the picture is drawn through the curve.

**Not tried in the game.** What the game does with the profile's `SETG` is the question: test T7 in [hc09_test_guide.md](hc09_test_guide.md) moves one receiver. If he lines up at the new place, alignment can be edited this way; if he lines up where the stock set has him, the game takes the on-field alignment from its own sets and the same edit would have to be made in the game files (`SETG` of `qkl_boot.ast` entry 40 and the compact copy in entry 42), a test series of its own.

## The play creator (Playbooks tab, Play creator...)

- **Three steps, in a strip at the top** (pick a play; click a player on the field and choose what he does; Make custom play), with a line that says what to do next. **Click a player on the field** to select him (he gets a ring; double-click opens the chooser); **Change what he does...** opens the simple chooser (see *The simple chooser*), and the old list of every assignment is behind *All assignments (advanced)*.
- Lists every play (Offense, Defense or Special teams), filtered by playbook, name or formation, option plays only; draws the alignment and the routes or zones, lists each slot's assignment in plain words ("route: 2 legs, 1 cut, then look for the ball", "pass block", "deep zone"; a code that is not understood shows as "step N"), with the game's own numbers underneath.
- **Change this slot...** opens the assignment library: every step list of that side, by kind of player, option reads only, only the lists no stock play uses, steps containing a text. A step list is drawn with the art the stock plays use with it most (or, for the lists no stock play uses, the pairing found in your profile's custom plays); a list with no known art cannot be used for a slot.
- **Make custom play...** (offense, for now; the button is off with a note for defense and special teams) opens the save window, whose one main button **Add to my game** writes the play, with the changed slots, into **copies** of a profile save and a career save (in `Documents\hc09-custom-play-output`, or a folder chosen under *Details*), checks them, saves your own two files in `Documents\hc09-test-backups` and puts the copies in place after one question (it refuses while RPCS3 runs or the open career has unsaved changes; *Put my saves back* undoes it). The editor finds the profile save next to the career, and chooses the custom playbook the chosen team's head coach plays when that is a custom one, else custom playbook 1 (the window says whether the team plays it); *Details* has the files, the output folder and the playbook, and *Only make the files* makes the copies without changing the game. Rows (`hc09_plays.build_custom_play`): `PBPL` and `SETL` in formation 8190 ("Created Plays", `SITT` 2 / 0), 11 `SETP` (the stock set's slots with its diagram coordinates in both `fmtx` / `artx` and `fmty` / `arty`), the stock set's `SGFF` groups with their `SETG` rows, 11 `PLYS`, and the `PLPD` / `PLRD` / `PLCM` row of the stock play; ids come after the profile's own (`SETL` 66 and `PLYL` 28 are next in the test profile). Every `PSAL` / `ARTL` is one of the game's own. The play is put into the chosen custom playbook 1 to 10 by `PGPL`, `PGSS`, `PGSL` rows (and a `PGFM` row if that playbook has no "Created Plays" formation yet), written to **both** saves: the profile holds them too (its 14 custom plays are linked by 29 `PGPL` rows, next to the playbooks' stock plays), so both are copied. The files are written with the bridge's append import (zero-filled slots), `verify-db` must pass on both.
- **Kind of play** is shown for every offensive play (from the quarterback's steps) with a check that every player's assignment is one the game ships in that kind. **Change play type...** lists the nine kinds with what the game's plays of each do, shows exactly which players would change (and from which stock play) and applies it. **Giving the quarterback an assignment that makes another kind** (for example a play-action one in a dropback pass) asks whether to bring the other players along; slots you changed by hand stay unless they cannot work. Make custom play writes the new kind's `PLYT` and its `PLPD` / `PLRD` / `PLCM` rows.
- The picture is drawn on the game's own field with the game's receiver numbers 1 to 5 for the pass reads (see *The game's own pictures*).
- **Pass reads...** (the in-game editor's progressions): the five skill players in the order the quarterback looks to them, each with the game's chance number (plays made in the game usually have 100, 20, 10, 5, 1); move receivers up and down and the field shows the game's numbers 1 to 5. They are written into the new play's own `PLPD` row in the profile, so no game file is touched. In all, 1,180 of the game's 1,184 pass plays have exactly this shape (the five skill slots `poso` 1 to 5, each once); what the chance does in play is the game's own number and is not decoded.
- **Drag a player on the field** to move him (offense): press on him, drag, let go; he stands where the mouse is, to a quarter yard and inside the field's limits, he is ringed in orange, and *Undo this change* puts him back (together with any change of his job). **Move players...** is the same thing in a window of its own, with the list, arrows and boxes for exact yards (see *Where the players line up*; untested in the game).
- Not in the creator yet: **the run hole** of a run play, **moving the pre-snap motion alignments**, and **new routes** (see above).

Tested on copies (`plays_test.py`, outside the repository): all 3,099 plays decode and draw; stock option plays are recognised; a custom play made from *Slants* with an option read in slot 2 writes 1 `PBPL`, 1 `SETL`, 11 `SETP`, 9 `SGFF`, 24 `SETG`, 11 `PLYS`, 1 `PLPD` and reads back with the same positions and the one changed assignment; the old rows are unchanged; a second play takes the next ids; the output folder is refused if it would overwrite the saves; the real profile, career and game files are byte-identical afterwards.

## Not known, and risks (what to watch in the first game test)

- **Whether the game accepts it.** The rows copy the layout of the plays the game wrote itself, but a field the editor does not understand (`PLRR` role codes, `risk`, `vpos`, `SITT`, `arti` / `opnm` / `tabo` / `flas` of `SETP`) could matter. Copies of the stock set's values are used wherever possible.
- **Option reads in a custom play.** `PLCM` (the play combination) is copied from the stock play, but whether the option decision needs more than the step lists and `PLCM` (the `PLPD` read order, the `PLRR` of the quarterback and the pitch man) is not known. A custom play with an option step list in a non-option play may do nothing or leave the quarterback standing.
- **Roles.** `PLRR` goes with the step list in the stock plays (the designer copies the most common one); mixing an assignment with a role it never has may confuse the AI.
- **Motion groups** (`SETG`): copied with the set, but a slot's assignment is not changed with the motion.
- **Kind conversion** is built from what the game ships, never tried in the game: a play turned into another kind uses assignments that the game's own plays of that kind have (from a play of that kind in the same set where there is one, 38% of the conversions tried), so the risk is in the combination (for example a receiver route kept in a play action), **in the 62% of conversions where no stock play of the kind uses the set** (the quarterback's, backs' and linemen's assignments then come from other formations, built for other alignments: drop depth in the shotgun against under center, mesh distances; the window says so and the first game test should use a conversion copied from a play of the same set, such as *Slants* in the set *Big*), and in the `PLPD` / `PLRD` rows that are made when no stock play of the kind uses the set (read order from the routes with the kind's usual percentages, the base play's hole).
- **Alignment.** A game test showed that `artx` / `arty` and `fmtx` do not move a player on the field (only in the diagram image), so a custom play lines up exactly as the stock set it was made from does, from the `SETG` yards that are copied with it; the designer cannot move a player yet. Changing `SETG` `x___` / `y___` (and entry 42's copy for the game files) is the next thing to test.
- **Ids.** Custom ids (`SETL` up to 511, `PLYL` up to 16,383) can equal stock ids; the game files its custom plays in the profile, and the career links by `SETL` / `PLYL`, as the game does for its own custom plays.
- **Defensive custom plays**: not made here (the profile's `PLAB` has `BOFF` for the playbook type; the rows would be the same on the defense tables).
- Names of the in-game route menu entries, what the 138 lists are in the in-game menu, and the exact meaning of each step code (the table above is read from the data).

## A test plan for the game

**For testers, the step-by-step version (what to click, what to look at, how to write it down, what each outcome means) is in [hc09_test_guide.md](hc09_test_guide.md); the editor has a Test plays window (seven plays made and added with one button, with the answers written in the same window; `hc09_playtest.py`), a Route test window and Add to my game / Put my saves back buttons for it.** The short plan:

1. Close RPCS3. Back up the **whole** profile folder (`BLUS30128-PROFILE-HEADCOACH`; there is one for every career, so the change reaches all of them and it holds all your real custom plays) and the career's folder. If you can, try it with a career you do not mind losing.
2. Play creator, pick *Slants*, change one receiver to an ordinary list (for example a deep route), **Make custom play...**, **Add to my game** (the editor makes the copies and puts them in place; or *Details*, *Only make the files* to do it by hand).
3. (By hand only: copy the two `USR-DATA` files over the profile's and the career's.) Start the game, load the career, open the playbook under *Created Plays*: the play should be listed with its name and the diagram of the set.
4. Run it. Look at: the alignment (the same as the stock set), the changed receiver's route, the others unchanged.
5. Then a change of kind: *Slants* made a play-action pass with Change play type..., and *Slants* made a handoff run. Look at: the quarterback (turns and fakes, or hands off), the linemen (run block first in play action), the back (fakes the handoff in play action, takes it in a run), the receivers (routes in a pass, blocks in a run), and that the play is called the right kind in the playbook screen (a pass has read numbers, a run shows the hole).
6. Only then try an option read in a pass play and a whole option play copy (*Speed Option* without changes, then with a changed back).
7. If anything is off, the useful reports are: does the play show in the playbook, does the diagram show, does the game crash on selecting or on snapping, and what the player did.

## Making your own routes and steps: what is possible

**Short answer: choosing among the game's own routes is easy and is built; adding a genuinely new route is not possible with the tools the editor has today, and rewriting one of the game's routes in place is possible but needs an in-game test of what the numbers mean first.** What was checked:

- **Where a custom play's assignments live.** A custom play's `PLYS` rows hold only ids (`PSAL`, `ARTL`), and the **profile save has no step-list or art tables at all** (its 43 tables include `PBPL`, `SETL`, `SETP`, `SETG`, `SGFF`, `PLYS`, `PLPD`, `PLRD`, `PLCM` and the playbook links, no `PSLO`, `PSLD`, `ARTO` or `ARTD`). So a custom play can only point at assignments that already exist in the game's own play database, which is what the in-game Create-A-Play does too.
- **The game's play database has no free room.** In `qkl_boot.ast` entry 40 (the only entry with step lists and art), `PSLO` has 5,581 rows of a capacity of 5,581, `PSLD` 1,449 of 1,449, `ARTO` 941 of 941, `ARTD` 487 of 487 (and `PLYS`, `PBPL`, `SETP`, `SETG`... likewise), and entry 42 is full too; the six smaller copies in `qkl_fe2ig.ast` (entries 1674 to 1679) have **no step-list or art tables at all** and their play tables are exactly full as well. (Measured with the table headers: `maxRecords` against `currentRecords`.) The editor's game-file tool writes **same-size** changes in place, so it can change the values in existing rows but cannot add a step list. Adding one would mean growing the database entry (the table, its header checksums, the entry's place in the archive): not built, never tried, and whether the game accepts it is unknown. **The art is separate from the steps (game test, Xbox 360 build):** editing the step numbers of the Post route changed what the receiver ran, but the play's picture on the play-call screen stayed the stock one in every test, so a route lab must write the matching art records (`ARTO` / `ARTD`, 12 segments per route, also exactly full) as well as the steps.
- **What can be rewritten in place.** A list can be rewritten if the new route has the same number of steps. The 138 lists no stock play uses are probably the in-game editor's menu (the user's custom plays use 22 of them), so they are not free to reuse. 251 of the 1,138 offensive lists are used by exactly one slot in the whole game; one of those could be overwritten, at the price of that one stock play's route changing.
- **A route has two halves that do not follow each other.** The steps (`8` legs, `9` cuts) are what the player does; the art is only the picture. The art cannot be computed from the steps: 2 legs go with 3 art points in 107 of the 375 distinct receiver routes, and the art squeezes long routes (a deep post is about 7 yards deep in it, its legs say far more). A new route would need art too, and the art tables are also full (53 of the 941 offensive art records are used by no stock play).
- **The numbers are not decoded.** What `val1` (a leg's length: 8 to 248, a go route 240), `val2` (32, 16, 48: pace or angle) and a cut's two numbers (direction, angle) mean in yards and degrees is unknown, and the art cannot teach it (previous point). Only a game test can: write a route with one number changed and watch the player.

**What the process would look like, as easy and intuitive as possible** (the first step is built; the others wait for the tests above):

1. **Pick by name (built).** *Change what he does* lists the game's routes by name and depth (Go, Slant, Post, Corner, Out, In, Curl, Hitch, Comeback, Drag, Flat, Wheel ...), each shown on the field with the player in place; nothing is written anywhere except the custom play. For nearly everything a coach wants this is enough: the game has 30 named route variants for each side of the field.
2. **Route lab (not built).** Draw the route with the mouse on the field (stem, break, finish) or drag the handles of a named route (depth, angle of the break); the editor says which of the game's routes it is closest to ("this is a Post, deep: the game has it") and uses that one, writing nothing. Only if you insist on a route the game does not have does it offer *Make it a new route*, which needs a route slot: it asks which of the 251 single-use stock routes to give up, shows which play loses it, makes the backup, writes the new steps (numbers copied from the closest stock route and scaled) and the nearest art, and says to restart the game.
3. **Calibrate first.** Before the lab can be trusted, one game test per number: rewrite a single-use route with the leg length doubled, then with the cut angle changed, and see what the receiver does. With those results the lab can turn a drawn line into numbers; without them it would be guessing.

## The simple chooser (how the names are made)

*Change what he does* (`hc09_jobs.py`) names every assignment the game's plays use for a kind of player, groups them (Routes, Blocks, Carry the ball, Option and pitch, Quarterback; on defense Pass rush, Blitz, Man coverage, Zones, Spy), and offers for a player standing where this one stands the version the game's plays use most for that side of the field (a route's art is drawn from the player's own spot, so a left-side slant is not a right-side slant). Choices the game does not use in this kind of play are hidden until asked for. The quarterback's list is one entry per kind of play (nine), followed by the **named options** the game's plays use for the two kinds that have several: dropbacks by how far he drops (stays in place, a short drop of about a yard; the usual deep drop of about six yards is the kind's own entry), a **rollout** to the right, and **play-action boots** to the right and to the left. They are told apart from the numbers the game stores (a move step is yards, heading, effort; the Scramble step is mode, yards, heading, where mode 1 is a passer on the move and the heading says which way: 0 is right, 64 left), and each is a step list some stock play already uses, so nothing is invented. The game's stock plays have no plain rollout to the left (only the right one, three plays); the game can flip a play, which is how a left one is made in play. Route names (`hc09_routes.py`) come from the shape of the route's art, read toward the sideline or the middle so that both sides give the same name; the depth words (short, medium, deep) are thirds of the art's own squeezed scale and are left off a go, a fade and a wheel, which are deep by definition; the break depths that tell a post from a slant, a curl from a hitch and an in from a drag were set from the names of the game's own plays. **Checked: in the plays named after a route (slant, post, corner, curl, hitch, drag, cross, flat, streak, go, dig, comeback, seam, out) at least one receiver has that route in 79% of 647 plays**, 100% for drags, 90% for "go" and 77% for "slants", but poorly for wheel (37%) and screen (24%), so the name is a good guide, not a promise: the picture beside the list is the check.

## The game's own pictures (the field and the receiver numbers)

The designer draws on the game's own Create-A-Play field and numbers the pass reads 1 to 5 with the game's own sprites. They are read from the user's own copy of the game at run time (`node bridge.js ui-assets --ast qkl_interface.ast --out-dir <cache>`, 0.3 seconds, run by the editor when the designer opens and cached in `.hc09_gamedata_cache/ui`, which is not part of the repository) and **none of the game's art is shipped with the editor**. Without `qkl_interface.ast` (or when the cache is missing) the designer draws on a plain green field as before.

Where they are: `qkl_interface.ast` (PS3 USA, 284 MB) holds 868 top-level entries, **each a nested `BGFA` archive that is one screen of the game's interface**: 20,419 sub-entries in all, 14,892 of them textures (DDS, mostly 256x256 DXT3; one archive alone holds 3,580), 1,642 `Apt` files (EA's Flash-style screen layouts, which contain names such as `createaplay`, `playcreator`, `playart`) and a few thousand small binary files. The Create-A-Play screen is entry **548**: eighteen textures, among them
- sub-entry 43, a **512x512 DXT field** (the green picture with the yard lines every 5 yards and the hash marks, its visible part in the top 480 x 276 pixels, measured from the pixels: the sidelines are the bright columns 6 to 16 and 477 to 487, the green ends at row 282);
- sub-entries 8 to 18, 32x32 sprites: a marker (a ring with a stem, 8) and the **red numbers 1 to 5** with a stem (10, 12, 14, 16, 18);
- the PlayStation button glyphs, the dotted menu background and some gradients.

The diagram's units map onto the field picture's visible part as x 0 to 180 across (480 pixels, so 2.667 pixels per unit and no resampling) and y 0 to 104 down (checked against the in-game thumbnails: a lineman at `arty` 68 stands 65% of the way down, as in the game). The numbers' sprites go above-left of the receiver, the stem touching him, as in the game; the pass reads are `PLPD`'s five `rcv` columns, in order, those with a percentage above 0.

The bridge finds the screen by what it contains, not by its position (a layout that mentions `createaplay`, one 512x512 texture and at least six 32x32 ones), with entry 548 as the place tried first, because other copies of the game order their entries differently (the same trap as for the shipped database). Its DXT1 / DXT3 / DXT5 decoder and PNG writer have no dependencies (Tk 8.6 loads PNG itself, so Pillow is not needed) and agree with Pillow's decode of the same textures to within 1 level of 255.

The player markers (the `T` for linemen, the square for the quarterback) are not among the pictures found and are drawn in code.
